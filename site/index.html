Two endpoints, deliberately separate.

POST /v1/posters
    Resolves a TMDB identifier to artwork, hashes the resolved
    specification, stores it, and returns an opaque key. Cheap: no
    image work happens here.

GET /v1/posters/{key}.webp
    Renders on first request, serves from cache after. Carries
    Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable.

A CDN will not cache a POST, and above a 90% hit rate the CDN is
doing most of the work — hence the split.

The key is blake3 over the resolved specification plus a renderer
version. Two consequences: requests differing only in ways the
renderer ignores land on one cache entry, and a rendering change
cannot be served from a stale one because it cannot produce the
same key.

Resolution happens at POST, not at render. If it happened at render
time the same URL would produce different pixels whenever TMDB
promoted a different poster, while its immutable header promised
otherwise.

Artwork is fetched from TMDB per render and never stored. The
service keeps what it produces, not what it consumes.
